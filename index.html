Engineering workflow

Design → Analyze → Build → Test → Iterate

The same loop runs through every project on this site. Each step below is a real moment from my own work.

  1. 01

    Design

    Parametric CAD assemblies and drawings in Fusion 360, Onshape and Siemens NX.

  2. 02

    Analyze

    Structural FEA in ANSYS Mechanical — stress, deflection and the regions that decide the design.

  3. 03

    Build

    Lathe work, sheet-metal brackets, frame fabrication and 3D-printed airframes.

  4. 04

    Test

    Competition, endurance runs and field tests on the real hardware.

  5. 05

    Iterate

    What bent, broke or weighed too much goes back into the next revision.

Iterate → back to design

01 / GP

Purdue ASME Racing · Aug 2025 – Present

Grand Prix Kart — Chassis & Steering

Chassis Project Lead. Front axle and spindle assembly — designed, analyzed, machined and taken to the Purdue Grand Prix.

The front end that carries steering input, braking and wheel loads on a competition go-kart: Fusion 360 CAD and GD&T drawings, ANSYS FEA on the spindle and tie rods, and lathe work on the shafts. Includes a tie-rod failure and redesign case file.

02 / UAV

Bravecore Private Limited · Kochi, India · Jun – Jul 2026

Tube-Launched UAV Airframe

Mechanical Engineering Intern. Lightweight structural design, from whiteboard sketch to printed airframe.

A surveillance drone that folds its arms to fit a launch tube, then deploys them. I built the parametric Onshape assembly — deployable arms, airframe, battery enclosure, electronics mounts and launch interface — and used ANSYS to take weight out without giving up strength or stiffness.
